# Risk factors and prevention of type 2 diabetes
Source: CDC "Diabetes Risk Factors" and "Preventing Type 2 Diabetes"; Diabetes Prevention Program (NEJM 2002) | https://www.cdc.gov/diabetes/risk-factors/index.html

## Risk factors
You are at higher risk of type 2 diabetes if you have prediabetes, are overweight or have obesity, are 45 or older, have a parent, brother or sister with type 2 diabetes, are physically active fewer than 3 times a week, have had gestational diabetes or given birth to a baby weighing over 9 pounds (4 kg), have non-alcoholic fatty liver disease, or have polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS). Some groups, including African American, Hispanic/Latino, American Indian, Alaska Native, Pacific Islander and some Asian American people, are at higher risk. High blood pressure, low HDL cholesterol, high triglycerides, smoking and poor sleep also add to risk.

## Ways to lower your risk
The most effective ways to prevent or delay type 2 diabetes and reduce your risk are: losing 5% to 7% of body weight if you are overweight, getting at least 150 minutes a week of moderate physical activity such as brisk walking, eating more vegetables, legumes and whole grains while cutting sugary drinks and refined carbohydrates, not smoking, sleeping enough, and getting your blood glucose checked regularly so prediabetes is caught early.

## Family history and genetics
Having a close relative with type 2 diabetes raises your risk, partly through shared genes and partly through shared eating and activity habits. Family history cannot be changed, but lifestyle changes still lower risk substantially for people with a strong family history.

## Prediabetes
Prediabetes often has no symptoms; in the United States about 1 in 3 adults has it and most do not know. Without changes, many people with prediabetes develop type 2 diabetes within 5 years, but prediabetes can often be reversed.

## The Diabetes Prevention Program
In the Diabetes Prevention Program trial, adults with prediabetes and excess weight who followed a lifestyle program aiming for 7% weight loss and at least 150 minutes a week of moderate activity such as brisk walking cut their chance of developing type 2 diabetes by 58% over about 3 years compared with placebo, and by 71% in people aged 60 or older. The medicine metformin lowered risk by 31%.

## Physical activity
Adults should aim for at least 150 minutes a week of moderate-intensity activity (for example brisk walking), or 75 minutes of vigorous activity, spread over the week, plus muscle-strengthening exercise on 2 or more days. Activity helps muscles use glucose and improves insulin sensitivity even without weight loss. Breaking up long periods of sitting also helps.

## Weight loss
For people with overweight or obesity, losing 5% to 7% of body weight (for example 5 to 7 kg for a 100 kg person) meaningfully lowers the risk of type 2 diabetes and improves blood glucose, blood pressure and cholesterol.

## Healthy eating
Eating patterns that lower diabetes risk emphasize vegetables, fruits, legumes, whole grains, nuts and unsaturated fats, and limit sugar-sweetened drinks, refined carbohydrates such as white bread and white rice, processed meat and highly processed foods. Watching portion sizes and choosing water over sugary drinks are simple first steps.

## Other habits
Smokers are 30% to 40% more likely to develop type 2 diabetes than non-smokers, so quitting lowers risk. Getting enough sleep (7 or more hours for most adults), limiting alcohol and managing stress also support healthy blood glucose.

## Gestational diabetes and later risk
Gestational diabetes usually goes away after the baby is born, but about half of women who had it go on to develop type 2 diabetes later. Testing after pregnancy, breastfeeding, staying active and keeping a healthy weight lower that risk.
