# Body weight, blood pressure and other measurements
Source: WHO body mass index classification; American Heart Association blood pressure categories | https://www.heart.org/en/health-topics/high-blood-pressure/understanding-blood-pressure-readings

## BMI categories
Body mass index (BMI) is weight in kilograms divided by height in metres squared. WHO classifies adult BMI as underweight below 18.5, normal weight 18.5 to 24.9, overweight 25 to 29.9, and obesity 30 or higher (class I 30 to 34.9, class II 35 to 39.9, class III 40 or higher). BMI does not distinguish fat from muscle, so it is a screening measure rather than a diagnosis.

## BMI in Asian populations
People of Asian descent, including South Asian (Indian, Pakistani, Bangladeshi, Sri Lankan) people, tend to develop type 2 diabetes at lower BMI levels. A WHO expert consultation suggested additional action points of 23 (increased risk) and 27.5 (high risk) for Asian populations, and the ADA recommends diabetes screening for Asian American adults from a BMI of 23.

## Waist size
Fat stored around the abdomen is especially linked to insulin resistance. A waist circumference above about 102 cm (40 inches) in men or 88 cm (35 inches) in women indicates higher risk; lower cut-offs (about 90 cm for men and 80 cm for women) are often used for South Asian people.

## Blood pressure categories
The American Heart Association classifies adult blood pressure as normal (below 120 systolic and below 80 diastolic), elevated (120 to 129 systolic and below 80 diastolic), stage 1 hypertension (130 to 139 systolic or 80 to 89 diastolic), and stage 2 hypertension (140 or higher systolic or 90 or higher diastolic). A reading above 180 systolic and/or above 120 diastolic is a hypertensive crisis that needs immediate care.

## Blood pressure and diabetes
High blood pressure is common in people with diabetes and together they sharply raise the risk of heart disease, stroke and kidney disease. The ADA generally recommends a blood pressure target below 130/80 mm Hg for people with diabetes. This app's dataset only records diastolic (the lower number) blood pressure.

## Skin fold thickness
Triceps skin fold thickness, measured with calipers on the back of the upper arm, estimates the amount of fat under the skin. Higher values reflect more body fat, which is linked to insulin resistance.

## Insulin levels
Serum insulin shows how much insulin the pancreas is releasing. In insulin resistance, the body often makes more insulin than usual to keep glucose normal, so high insulin can be an early warning sign; later in type 2 diabetes, insulin output can fall. Reference ranges depend on the laboratory and the timing of the test, so results should be read with a clinician.
